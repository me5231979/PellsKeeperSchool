Our philosophy

Coached from the goal, not the sideline

Today’s keeper is the first line of attack and the last line of defense. Our coaches train keepers in the goal, in game-like situations, building the technical skill and tactical sense the modern position demands. Small groups of 5–6 by skill level mean every keeper gets real reps and individual attention.

  • Shot stopping

    Set position, handling, and catching under pressure.

  • Diving

    Safe, powerful technique from the ground up.

  • 1v1s

    Closing down, timing, and winning breakaways.

  • Crosses

    Reading flight, starting position, and claiming high balls.

  • Distribution

    Goal kicks, punts, throws, and playing out of the back.

  • Game sense

    Positioning, communication, and organizing a back line.

We also train the physical preparation and the mental side of the game, so we develop the whole player.

Founder & director · Since 2007

Laurie Pells George

Laurie founded Pells Keeper School in 2007 as a complete goalkeeping resource. She has coached at every level, from youth soccer to national teams, with 15 years in college soccer at Maryland, Clemson and The College of New Jersey.

Questions

Before you register

Does my child need goalkeeper experience?

Not for most camps. We group keepers by skill level, so beginners and experienced keepers each train at the right pace. Winter Boot Camp is for experienced keepers in grade 8 and up.

Who coaches the camps?

Camps are directed by Laurie Pells George. She is a U.S. Youth National Team goalkeeper coach and a former pro keeper. Her staff are current and former college goalkeepers with years of coaching experience.

How big are the groups?

5–6 keepers per coach-led group, sorted by skill level.

What should my keeper bring?

Goalkeeper gloves, cleats, shin guards, a full water bottle, and sunscreen. For full-day camps, pack a bagged lunch.
